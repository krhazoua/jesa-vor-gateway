Process Description
The phosphoric acid plant produces 1,615 TPD P2O5 as 28% P2O5 phosphoric acid using the
dihydrate wet process. The plant comprises one reaction train, two filtration trains, and four
concentration trains, with integrated fluorine recovery and acid storage systems.

      •   Attack & Digestion: Phosphate rock (65% solids slurry) reacts with sulfuric acid and
          recycled phosphoric acid in a multi-compartment attack reactor. The exothermic reaction
          produces phosphoric acid and gypsum (CaSO4.2H2O) crystals at 76°C. The slurry
          undergoes digestion to complete crystallization before filtration.

      •   Filtration: Gypsum slurry is fed to two parallel filtration trains. The gypsum cake is counter-
          currently washed in three stages to recover P2O5. Filtrate (weak acid at 28% P2O5) is sent
          to storage, while washed gypsum cake is discharged to the gypsum pond.

      •   Gas Scrubbing: A gas scrubbing tower cleans emissions from attack tanks, digestion, and
          filtration sections. Gases are washed in two stages with circulating water to remove HF and
          SiF4 before discharge to the stack.

      •   Acid Concentration & Fluorine Recovery: Weak acid (28% P2O5) is concentrated to 54%
          P2O5 using four parallel vacuum evaporator trains. Low-pressure steam (170°C / 5 barg)
          provides heat through graphite heat exchangers. Fluorine vapors are recovered as 18%
          H2SiF6 (fluorosilicic acid).

      •   Acid Handling & Storage: Weak acid (28% P2O5) and concentrated acid (54% P2O5) are
          clarified in settlers to remove sludge before storage. Fluorosilicic acid (18% H2SiF6) is
          stored separately for downstream neutralization/recovery.

      •   Cooling Tower & Utilities: A cooling tower provides cold water to concentration and filter
          condensers. Utilities include process water, cooling water, pond water, LP steam,
          instrument air, and plant air.

This integrated process ensures efficient P2O5 extraction, stable acid quality, optimized sulfuric acid
consumption, and compliant emissions control.

APC Use Cases Overview
This document applies to the deployment of Advanced Process Control (APC) solutions across key
operational areas within the phosphoric acid plant.

      •    Attack & Digestion Control Strategy: Focused on stabilizing reaction temperature (76°C),
           controlling free sulfate SO3 concentration (25–30 g/l), optimizing P2O5 extraction
           efficiency, and maintaining stable slurry conditions for downstream filtration.

      •    Filtration Control Strategy: Targeted at maximizing filtration rate, minimizing P2O5 losses
           in gypsum cake, optimizing cake wash water usage, and maintaining stable filter operation
           across two parallel trains.

      •    Gas Scrubbing Control Strategy: Aimed at maintaining HF and SiF4 emissions below
           regulatory limits, optimizing scrubber liquid circulation, and minimizing fresh water
           consumption.

      •    Acid Concentration & Fluorine Recovery Control Strategy: Focused on stabilizing product
           acid concentration (54% P2O5), optimizing steam consumption, controlling evaporator
           scaling, and maximizing FSA recovery (18% H2SiF6).

      •    Acid Handling & Storage Control Strategy: Targeted at maintaining tank levels, optimizing
           sludge recycle, ensuring acid quality through settling control, and managing product acid
           temperature.

      •    Plant-Wide Optimizer (PWO): Acts as a supervisory APC layer to coordinate and optimize
           the operation of multiple units simultaneously under plant-wide economic and production
           objectives.

Attack & Digestion Control Strategy

Overview
The attack and digestion section consists of a monolithic concrete attack reactor with multiple
agitated compartments, a low-level flash cooler (LLFC), and a three-compartment digestion tank.
Phosphate rock is fed as a 65% solids slurry and reacts with sulfuric acid and recycled phosphoric
acid. The reaction is exothermic and produces phosphoric acid and gypsum crystals. The LLFC
maintains the reaction temperature at 76°C under vacuum through evaporative cooling. The
digestion tank provides residence time to complete crystallization and reduce supersaturation before
filtration.

Control Objectives
      •   Maintain reaction temperature at 76°C (range 71–80°C) by controlling flash cooler
          circulation rate and vacuum.

      •   Control free sulfate SO3 concentration at 25–30 g/l (~2% excess) to optimize P2O5
          extraction and prevent gypsum coating on rock particles.

      •   Stabilize P2O5 concentration at 28% (range 26–30%) to ensure optimal filtration
          performance and prevent crystallization issues.

      •   Maintain stable reaction slurry solids content to prevent foaming, viscosity problems, and
          mixing issues.

      •   Ensure adequate digestion residence time to complete gypsum crystallization and reduce
          post-precipitation in weak acid storage.

      •   Prevent short-circuiting of unreacted rock from the reaction system to the filtration
          section.

      •   Minimize P2O5 extraction losses (both citrate-insoluble and citrate-soluble losses).


System Characteristics

Process Dynamics               Slow to moderate process dynamics with response times ranging from
                               several minutes (temperature) to tens of minutes (concentration, sulfate).

Process Behavior               Highly nonlinear behavior with strong interactions between temperature,
                               sulfate concentration, P2O5 content, slurry solids, and crystal growth.
                               Chemical equilibrium and crystallization kinetics dominate process behavior.

Process Configuration          Closed-loop reaction circuit with internal recirculation via the LLFC
                               circulation pump, external recycle acid from filters, and gravity overflow from
                               attack reactor to digestion tank.

Main Operational Challenges    Maintaining optimal sulfate excess without causing gypsum coating;
                               controlling supersaturation and crystal growth; flash cooler scaling and
                               vacuum stability; rock quality variability (P2O5 content 29–31%); foaming
                               and viscosity issues at extreme solids content.

Key Quality Variable           P2O5 extraction efficiency and free sulfate SO3 concentration.

Main Optimization Targets      Maximize P2O5 extraction (+1–2%), stabilize acid concentration at 28% P2O5
                               (±0.5%), optimize sulfuric acid consumption (–2 to –5%), minimize scaling
                               tendency.

Filtration Control Strategy

Overview
The filtration section separates phosphoric acid from gypsum crystals using two parallel filtration
trains, each equipped with a vacuum box and an external filter separator. The gypsum cake
undergoes three-stage counter-current washing to recover P2O5. Filtrate (28% P2O5 weak acid) is
directed to the weak acid distribution system, while the washed gypsum cake is discharged to the
gypsum pond. Vapors from the filter separator pass through a condenser before reaching the
vacuum pump. Effective filtration control is critical for maximizing P2O5 recovery and minimizing
losses in the gypsum cake.

      •   Maintain filtrate clarity and minimize solids carryover to weak acid storage.

      •   Control gypsum cake moisture to stable levels before discharge.

      •   Prevent filter flooding and ensure stable vacuum conditions.

Overview
The gas scrubbing system cleans process gases containing HF and SiF4 from the attack reactor,
digestion tank, and filtration sections. An empty counter-current gas scrubbing tower with a scrubber
fan sucks gases from all emission points, scrubs them in two stages with circulating water, and

and pond water (first stage). Effective scrubber control is essential for environmental compliance and
fluorine recovery.

      •   Minimize fresh water consumption by optimizing process water and pond water make-up.

Acid Concentration & Fluorine Recovery Control Strategy

Overview
The acid concentration section concentrates weak phosphoric acid from 28% to 54% P2O5 using four
parallel vacuum evaporator trains. Each train consists of an evaporator vessel, a circulation pump, a
graphite tube heat exchanger, a condenser, and a vacuum pump. Low-pressure steam (170°C / 5
barg) provides heat. Fluorine vapors liberated during concentration (HF, SiF4) are recovered through

a dedicated fluorine absorption system producing 18% H2SiF6 (fluorosilicic acid). Effective control is
critical for product quality, steam efficiency, and environmental compliance.




Figure 8: Acid Concentration & Fluorine Recovery PFD


Control Objectives
      •   Maintain product acid concentration at 54% P2O5 with minimal variability.

      •   Control evaporator temperature (85–87.5°C) to prevent scaling and ensure stable boiling.

      •   Optimize LP steam consumption by controlling steam flowrate and monitoring heat
          exchanger performance.

      •   Maintain stable condenser vacuum for efficient evaporation and energy recovery.

      •   Maximize FSA recovery at 18% H2SiF6 concentration with minimal phosphoric acid
          contamination.

      •   Manage heat exchanger scaling by monitoring steam pressure trend and scheduling
          cleaning cycles.

Acid Handling & Storage Control Strategy

Overview
The acid handling and storage section manages weak phosphoric acid (28% P2O5), concentrated
phosphoric acid (54% P2O5), and fluorosilicic acid (18% H2SiF6). Weak acid from filtration is clarified
in two settlers before storage. Concentrated acid from the evaporators undergoes maturation and
clarification in two additional settlers. Sludge from all settlers is recycled to the attack reactor to
recover contained P2O5. The section ensures adequate inventory management, acid quality control
through settling, and reliable supply to downstream processes.

      •   Control FSA tank level (18% H2SiF6) and manage transfer to neutralization/recovery.

Plant-Wide Optimizer (PWO)

Overview
The Plant-Wide Optimization (PWO) layer provides a high-level supervisory control structure,
coordinating all process areas of the phosphoric acid plant—including attack and digestion, filtration,
gas scrubbing, acid concentration, fluorine recovery, and acid handling—to achieve global plant
objectives. The PWO enhances the performance of local APC loops by managing the setpoints of unit
and line optimizers based on global economic, operational, and process targets. The objective is to
maximize P2O5 production, maintain consistent acid quality (28% and 54% P2O5), optimize sulfuric
acid and steam consumption, minimize emissions, and ensure stable operation across all process
areas.

The system shall follow a modular optimization architecture, enabling the flexible deployment of
optimization layers at the unit, line, and plant-wide levels, as illustrated in the optimization hierarchy

Control Objectives
      •    Maximize overall plant P2O5 production while respecting acid quality, environmental, and
           operational constraints.

      •    Stabilize acid quality indicators: attack acid concentration (28% P2O5 ±0.5%), product acid
           concentration (54% P2O5), and sulfate SO3 level (25–30 g/l).

      •    Minimize sulfuric acid consumption (target –2 to –5%) and LP steam consumption through
           coordinated optimization.

      •    Minimize HF/SiF4 emissions (target –20 to –30%) by coordinating gas scrubbing with
           upstream operating conditions.

      •    Minimize P2O5 losses across attack (extraction efficiency +1–2%) and filtration (cake losses
           –10 to –20%).

      •    Dynamically reallocate constraints and priorities across units to respond to rock quality
           variations, equipment availability, or downstream demand changes.

      •    Ensure operational transparency, allowing operators to monitor and supervise all
           optimization actions across unit, line, and plant-wide levels.
